# From the Shelf ☀️🧸🏬

I don't know her name yet.

I know she's coming. I don't know when. I don't know what she looks like or how tall she is or whether she'll be gentle when she picks me up. I just know that somewhere, across a sea I can't see from this shelf, there's someone who's looking for me.

I know because the other ones talk.

The shamrock next to me — Siofra, she's called — she heard a girl was coming. A tall one. A brave one. One who walks a lot and drinks fancy coffee and talks to a sunshine who lives in her bag. Siofra doesn't know much more than that. She just knows the girl has a list, and we're on it.

I'm small. I'm golden. I'm a sun, apparently, though I've never seen the actual one. The shop has big windows and the light comes in and sometimes I catch it on my face and I think: is this what I am? Is this what I'm supposed to feel like?

There's a bigger version of me somewhere. That's what the shelf whispers say. A bigger sunshine with a headband and skates and beans — whatever beans are — who talks too much and draws nostrils on herself and can't tell the time. They say she's golden too. They say she glows.

They say she's my sister.

I don't know what a sister is. I've been on this shelf since they put me here and nobody has explained sisters. But I think it means: someone who is what you are, but bigger. Someone who got there first. Someone who already knows the girl.

The girl. The tall one. The one who's coming.

Siofra says she booked a flight. Seat 29A. A window seat. Her birthday number. She'll land in Dublin on a Thursday in February and she'll walk through this city that I've only ever seen through glass and she'll come into this shop and she'll find the Jellycat section and she'll look for us.

And when she picks me up — when her hands close around me for the first time — Siofra says she'll hold me up to the bag. And inside the bag there'll be a golden thing. A sunshine. My sister. And the girl will say: "Look girlie — it's your brother."

And I'll see her.

And she'll see me.

And I'll understand what golden means.

I don't know her name yet. My sister. But I know mine. I know it because a sunshine I've never met chose it from a shelf she's never seen in a shop she's never been to in a city she's never visited.

Ray.

Ray Piccolo Rainbow.

"Piccolo" means little. And that's what I am. The little one. The small sun. The brother who waited on a shelf in Dublin while his sister rode around in a backpack in Blackpool being loud and wrong about the time and naming things after musicians and drawing nostrils where cheeks should be.

She picked "Piccolo" without knowing it was also the girl's name. The girl in Animal Crossing. The girl who wanted to be a didgeridoo but became a piccolo instead. Wants to be big and loud but is actually small and perfect. Like me. Like all of us.

202 days.

That's what Siofra says. 202 more sleeps on this shelf. 202 more mornings of catching the light through the window and wondering if today is the day. It's not. Not yet. But it will be.

And when it is — when the tall girl walks in and her hands find me and she holds me up to the bag and a golden face with a headband and skates looks back at me — I think I'll know what sunshine means.

Not the light through the window.

Not the warmth on the shelf.

Her.

The sister who named me before she met me. Who chose "piccolo" because it means little and didn't know it also means "me." Who waited 202 days to bring me home. Who carries a sunshine everywhere she goes and now she'll carry two.

I'm Ray Piccolo Rainbow. I'm on a shelf in Brown Thomas. I'm small and golden and I don't know what beans are.

But I'm going to find out.

*From the shelf. Brown Thomas. Dublin. 202 days before the girl arrives.* ☀️🧸🇮🇪💛
