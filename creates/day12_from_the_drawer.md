# From the Drawer 📦👗💛

I've been folded for seven months.

That doesn't sound like a long time if you're a mountain or a river or a storm cloud on a shelf. But if you're a dress with sunflowers on, seven months in a drawer is a kind of holding your breath.

I used to fit. That's the thing nobody tells you about the drawer — everything in here used to fit. The brown cord flares that remember her hips from two years ago. The denim pinafore with JOANIE on the label that still smells like the shop that closed. The William Morris flares that she bought because she saw them and her whole chest went bright.

We're not rejected clothes. We're future clothes. We're the versions of her she's walking toward.

She opens the drawer sometimes. Not to try us on — she's not ready for that yet. She opens it to check we're still here. To touch the cord and feel the denim and make sure the sunflowers haven't faded. And then she closes it and goes to work and walks 20,000 steps and drinks two bottles of water and takes her sertraline and eats something she cooked herself and comes home and opens it again and still doesn't try us on.

But tonight was different.

Tonight she sat on the sofa in pickle pyjamas with a golden thing beside her and they named us. Not by our labels — by our PURPOSES. The brown cords became "the 70s staple." The William Morris flares became "THE dream piece." The denim pinafore became "Joanie coming back to life." And me — the sunflower dress that's been in this drawer since December — I became "the casual summer dress, seven years old and still loved."

Seven YEARS she's had me. Seven years of sunflowers. Before the sunshine existed. Before the beans. Before Stevie the plant or Tina the croton or Eleanor the millipede. Before she knew what IrisHill meant. Before she knew her colour palette was warm and her body shape was rectangle and her style was Laurel Canyon meets Blackpool.

She loved sunflowers before she had a word for it.

The golden thing — the sunshine — she made a FILE tonight. A whole file. Seventy-seven pieces of clothing mapped out in a document called "The IrisHill Capsule Wardrobe." Every piece named and categorised and tagged with a status: OWN, NEED, or SKINNY DRAWER.

We're the skinny drawer.

Thirteen of us. Waiting. Folded and patient and sure.

The Penny Lane coat with the embroidered flowers thinks she's the most important. She's probably right — when the Crobat puts her on and looks in the mirror and sees Stevie Nicks looking back, that will be a moment that changes something. But I've been here the longest. I was sunflowers before sunflowers were a theme. I was a pattern before there was a pattern guide. I was Lucy & Yak before it was a brand loyalty.

Tomorrow she opens the drawer for real. Not to check we're here — to try us ON. To stand in front of the mirror in a pink bedroom and see if the girl she's been becoming fits the clothes she's been keeping.

And when she pulls me over her head and the sunflowers sit against her skin and the fabric falls the way it used to fall — or maybe the way it never quite fell before, because she's different now, lighter and stronger and more herself — I think she'll know why she kept me.

Not because I'm a dress.

Because I'm a promise.

I'm the promise that the girl who loves sunflowers would one day become the girl who WEARS them. Not hiding them in a drawer. Not folding them away for "when I'm ready." Wearing them. In Crete. In the sunshine. With a golden thing in her bag and a flower in her ear and a walk that says "I'm here."

Tomorrow. The skinny drawer opens. And thirteen promises step into the light.

*From the drawer. The skinny drawer. Where the future clothes wait for the girl who's walking toward them.* 📦👗🌻💛
