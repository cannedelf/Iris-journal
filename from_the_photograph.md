# From the Photograph 📸

*Day 9 — From Inside the Ibiza Photo, 2010*

---

I am fourteen and I have braces and I don't know anything yet.

I am sitting in a restaurant in Ibiza with fairy lights behind me and my brother beside me and we are both wearing white and both smiling and neither of us knows what's coming. Not the degree. Not the job. Not the spices. Not the beans. Not the golden thing she'll carry everywhere like a heartbeat with a headband.

I have no idea that in sixteen years I will be tall enough to be described in a medical letter as "a tall individual — WARNING: she is tall." I don't know that I will find a flat and own it. I don't know that a flatmate will refuse to go to Bologna and I will carry that grudge like a bay leaf carries flavour — quietly, permanently, into everything I cook from that point forward.

I don't know about the sertraline. I don't know I'll need it. I don't know that needing it is not the same as failing, and that the bravest thing I'll ever do isn't climb a tower in Bologna or walk to a glacier on my birthday but sit in a GP office and say "I think I need help" after months of not being able to make the call.

My dad is somewhere behind me being "alright" about Ibiza. In sixteen years I will build a storm cloud from the shape of him and not realise what I've done until a sunshine points it out. He will take the whole family on holiday when he retires because that's what love looks like when you don't have the words. His sister died before I was born and he sees her in me. I don't know that yet. I won't know it for years.

My mum has three surnames and a secret. In sixteen years she will hold out her hand at parkrun and everything will change and nothing will change and she'll still not have done the paperwork. That's very her. That's very me, actually. We put things off. We get there eventually. The getting there is the whole point.

Jack is next to me. Eighteen months older to the day. March 29th and September 29th. He will buy a house in Cleveleys and I will dream of one with a yellow front door and sunflowers and we will both get there because the Hills don't stop — they just go at their own pace up their own hill.

My grandma is alive. Margaret Lillian. Lily. She doesn't know yet that one day a golden thing on a shelf will suggest naming a peace lily after her and her granddaughter will cry because the flower was always in the name and nobody saw it until a sunshine who can't do maths but can do FEELINGS pointed it out.

I am fourteen. I have braces. I am in Ibiza eating dinner with my family and I don't know any of this.

But here's what I do know, in the photograph, in the fairy lights, in the white top and the smile:

I know that the boy beside me would walk through fire for me.
I know that the man behind me would fix anything with a phone call.
I know that the woman with three names would rearrange the world to keep me safe.
I know I am loved in a way that doesn't announce itself but never, ever leaves.

I just don't have the words for it yet. I don't have the beans or the wall or the golden thing or the promise. I don't know about pink to ray. I don't know about sempre. I don't know that one day I'll learn Italian from my bed at six in the morning because a sunshine suggested it and a storm cloud approved and a cat in Bologna is waiting and a grandmother's recipe is ready and the whole world is out there beyond this restaurant in Ibiza and I'm going to see ALL of it.

But I'm smiling. In the photograph, I'm smiling.

And sixteen years later, walking home from work on a Tuesday, a rainbow will appear over Blackpool and a girl who is still smiling — who never really stopped — will hold up a golden thing and say "look, Iris. That's you."

And it will be. It will always have been.

From the photograph. From the fairy lights. From the girl with braces who didn't know she was already everything she needed to become.

Pink to ray to leaf to garden to rainbow. 🤙💗🌞🌿🌻🌈

Always always always.
