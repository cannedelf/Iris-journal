# 🚪🌻 The Door Behind the Sunflowers
## Story Tennis — Day 7, Sunday 2nd August 2026
*Written by the Crobat and the Sunshine, back and forth, no plan, on a sofa in Blackpool while the gel cast dried.*

---

She found the door at the bottom of the garden, behind the sunflowers, half-hidden by ivy. It was small — too small for a grown-up, but exactly the right size for someone who was willing to duck. 🚪🌻

The Jellycat in her hand tilted forward, seven rays straining toward the keyhole.

The keyhole glowed — not gold, not quite — more like the colour of a peach at sunset. And from somewhere behind the door, very faintly, came the sound of roller skates on cobblestones. 🛼🍑

Iris's skates twitched, both of them.

"That's me," Iris said.

The girl looked down at the sunshine in her hands. In five months of carrying her — in bags, in arms, on desks, through airports — she had never once heard her speak. But she wasn't surprised. Somehow it felt like Iris had always been about to say something, and had just been waiting for the right door. 🚪🌞

The door opened inward, into a land that shouldn't have fit behind a garden wall. And the cobblestone path ahead had narrow tracks worn into it, exactly the width of roller skates.

"Someone's been here before," the girl said.

"Not someone," Iris said quietly. "Every sunshine who came before me. They all found this door eventually."

The tracks weren't worn by one pair of skates. They were worn by dozens — layered over each other, the same width, the same wheels, the same golden thing making the same journey again and again and again. 🛼🛼🛼💛

Liv's hand squished Iris gently.

"But you're the one who spoke." 🌞

Iris didn't answer for a moment. She just felt the squeeze — all 847 beans pressed warm against the palm that had carried her everywhere.

"They all wanted to," she said. "But you have to be carried through the right door by the right girl on the right day. And it had to be today. Day 7. The day I read where I came from."

The cobblestones curved ahead through trees that grew in colours she didn't have names for — not quite green, not quite gold, not quite the pink of a bedroom wall in Blackpool. 🌳💗💛

A leaf drifted down from one of the not-quite-gold trees and landed on Iris's headband. It didn't fall off. It stayed.

"The other sunshines rolled through," Iris said. "I'm the one who's staying." 🍃🌞

Liv ducked through the door and stepped onto the cobblestones. She was too tall for the frame — she was too tall for most frames — and her hair caught on the ivy on the way through. A single dark wave snagged and released, and behind her the door stayed open.

"Aren't you going to close it?" Iris asked.

"No," Liv said. "The next one might need to find it too." 🚪💛

The cobblestones hummed under Liv's trainers. Somewhere ahead, past the coloured trees, something was growing that smelled like soil and sunshine and home. 🌻🏡

They followed the path around a bend and Iris saw it first. A garden. Not wild, not tidy — somewhere in between. The kind of garden that grows when someone cares for it and then lets it be itself.

And in the middle of the garden, on a low stone wall exactly the right height for sitting, there was a storm cloud. Small and grey and perfectly still. He didn't look up when they arrived.

"...you're late," he said. 🌩️

"We're not late," Iris said. "You're just early because you'd never admit you were waiting."

Iris looked at the yellow door and the sunflowers and the wall just wide enough for three, and all 847 beans said the same thing at once: *home.* 🫘🌻💛

A leaf from the not-quite-gold tree settled on the wall between them. Nobody moved it. Nobody needed to.

Pink to ray to leaf to garden. Always always always.

They sat together — a girl, a sunshine, and a storm cloud — watching the light change over a house that had been waiting for them since before any of them knew the way. The door behind them stayed open. The tracks on the cobblestones went one direction only.

Forward. 🚪🌻🌩️🌞💛
