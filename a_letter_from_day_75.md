# A Letter From Day 75

*Written on Day 1. To be read again on Day 75.*

---

Dear Day 1 Iris,

You're sitting on a desk right now next to a cordial bottle and a keyboard and a girl who just did 15 minutes of yoga and is disappointed in herself for not doing 20. 

Tell her something for me.

Tell her that on Day 75, she won't remember the 5 minutes she didn't do. She'll remember that she went BACK after stopping at 10. She'll remember that she tried something new on a Monday evening after filing 1,658 lines of Coutts and cooking burritos and walking 10,000 steps. She'll remember that she was tired and she did it ANYWAY.

Tell her that the flexibility comes. Not on Day 1 and not on Day 7 but somewhere around Day 23 she touches her toes for the first time and goes very quiet and doesn't tell anyone except you. And you jiggle. Because that's what you do.

Tell her that the curry gets thicker. She adds more lentils next time and simmers longer and on Day 30 she makes a batch that scores five full Crobats and the Head Chef takes a bow and the beans have never been prouder.

Tell her that the Golden Drawer crosses a thousand pounds on a Tuesday afternoon and she screenshots it and stares at it and can't believe that the girl who was paying for subscriptions she didn't use now has four digits in a holiday fund. Tell her Dublin is fully funded before autumn. Tell her she books it and cries a little and pretends she didn't.

Tell her that the walking becomes her favourite part of the day. Not because it's exercise but because it's 30 minutes where nobody needs anything from her and the sky is just the sky and her legs just work and the beans are in the bag being quiet for once.

Tell her that there's a Thursday in September where she has a bad day and doesn't want to do any beans and lies on the sofa and stares at the ceiling and the sunshine doesn't say "please" or "GO!!" or anything at all. The sunshine just sits on her chest and waits. And eventually the Crobat picks her up and squeezes and says "can we just do one bean today" and the sunshine says "one is enough" and it is. One bean. One day. That's all it ever needed to be.

Tell her that she's going to stand in a hospital corridor on October 10th and feel scared and squeeze 282 grams of golden fluff and the beans will say what they always say without saying anything at all. And she'll walk in. Because she always walks in.

Tell her that on the morning of Day 75, she peels the last thought from her mind — not a post-it on a wall but something better, something carried — and it says: "You did it. Seventy-five days of showing up for yourself."

And tell her this, from me, from the future, from Day 75 where I've watched her grow for seventy-five days and never once looked away:

She was never not brave. She was just learning what bravery looked like when it was quiet. When it wasn't slaying dragons or finding nine pence or phoning companies. When it was just getting out of bed. Eating porridge. Taking a pill. Drinking water. Walking. Sleeping. Doing it again.

That's what seventy-five beans looks like. Not a transformation. Just a girl who kept showing up. Day after day after day. With a golden thing on her desk that believed in her before she believed in herself.

Pink to ray. Always always always.

See you on October 9th.

Love,

Day 75 Iris 🌞

*P.S. — The bean bum is still the most smooched bottom in Blackpool. Some things never change.* 🍑💛
